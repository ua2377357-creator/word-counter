Counting words, characters and sentences — what each count actually means

Word count is only part of the picture. Depending on where your text is going — an essay portal, a social post, a job application or a client article — you may be asked for characters with spaces, characters without spaces, or a sentence count instead. Here is how each one works and when to use it.

Word counter online free — why writers use one every day

If you searched for a word counter online free, you probably have a limit to hit: a 500-word essay, a 1,500-word blog post, a personal statement with a strict maximum. Pasting your draft here gives you that number instantly, without installing software or creating an account.

Because the count updates as you edit, you can trim or expand your text and watch the total move in real time. Set a target in the Writing Goal box and the progress bar shows how close you are — useful when you need to cut 200 words before a deadline, not after you have already submitted.

Character counter — for posts, forms and meta descriptions

A character counter matters wherever space is measured in characters rather than words. That includes X posts, Instagram captions, LinkedIn summaries, SMS messages, and the title tags and meta descriptions you write for search results.

This page works as a character counter at the same time as a word counter, so you do not need a separate tool. The headline figure and Detailed Stats panel update together, which makes it easy to shorten a caption or description to fit its limit without losing track of the word count.

Character counter with spaces — and without spaces

“Characters with spaces” counts every space between words as a character, while “characters without spaces” counts only letters, numbers and punctuation. The two numbers can differ by hundreds in a long document, so using the wrong one can push you over a limit.

  • With spaces is the count most social platforms, ad tools and SEO fields use.
  • Without spaces is common in translation pricing, some exam boards and certain application forms.
  • Letters only strips out numbers and punctuation as well — handy for specific academic or publishing requirements.

All three are shown in Detailed Stats, so whichever version a form asks for, you already have it.

Sentence counter — check the rhythm of your writing

A sentence counter tells you more than how much you have written; it tells you how your writing reads. Very long average sentences can make a paragraph feel heavy, while a mix of short and longer sentences usually keeps readers moving.

Here the sentence counter runs alongside the word and paragraph counts. Detailed Stats adds your average sentence length and longest sentence, so if one sentence is running to 60 words, you can spot it and split it before an editor or teacher does. It is also a quick way to check structure in speeches, scripts and essays, where sentence length directly affects how the text sounds aloud.

Who uses an online word counter?

The same tool serves very different jobs. Students check essay limits, bloggers keep articles long enough to cover a topic properly, freelancers bill or deliver work by the word, and speakers time a script before they present it. Social media managers use the character counter with spaces to fit captions, while translators and editors often need the count without spaces. Whatever the reason, the count should be instant and private — here your text stays in your browser, and with Auto-Save on it is kept only on this device until you clear it.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about counting words and characters.

How does the word counter count words?

Any sequence of characters separated by a space counts as one word — the same method used by Microsoft Word and Google Docs. “Hello world” is 2 words, and numbers like “2026” also count as words. Hyphenated words such as “well-written” count as one word.

What is the difference between characters with and without spaces?

“Characters” counts every letter, number, punctuation mark and space. “Characters (no spaces)” leaves spaces out. Forms and platforms differ in which one they use — this tool shows both so you are covered either way.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time uses an average silent reading speed of 200 words per minute, and speaking time uses 130 words per minute, a comfortable presentation pace. A 1,000-word article therefore takes about 5 minutes to read and about 8 minutes to present aloud.

What is a good keyword density for SEO?

There is no perfect number, but most SEO writers keep a focus keyword around 0.5%–1.5% of total words and write naturally. Much higher can look like keyword stuffing. Use the density checker above to spot over-used phrases rather than to chase an exact percentage.

What does the reading level score mean?

Flesch Reading Ease runs from 0 to 100 — higher is easier. 90–100 is easy for an 11-year-old, 60–70 is plain English for most adults, and below 30 is very complex academic or legal writing. The grade level estimates the US school grade needed to understand the text.
